# Bowling knowledge for TrueLine

Written Nov 15, 2026. A working reference for product, UX and copy decisions, so
that what TrueLine says sounds like it came from someone who bowls on a league
or college team. Right-handed terms are used throughout; a left-hander is the
exact mirror (boards count from the left gutter, the pocket is the 1-2).
Sources are linked inline. Where a source only appeared in a search snippet or
is no longer online, that is noted.

## 1. How bowlers describe a shot

A competitive bowler describes a shot as a set of board numbers, not a curve.
"I'm standing 35, looking at 12, getting it out to 7 at about 40 feet" is a
complete description. The pieces are:

- **Stand board (feet).** The board the inside of the slide foot is on at the
  start of the approach, read off the approach dots. Bowlers also talk about
  where the slide foot finishes at the foul line, because drift (walking left or
  right) changes the line without them meaning to.
- **Laydown board.** Where the ball first touches the lane past the foul line.
  It sits several boards to the ball side of the slide foot, and the gap
  depends on the bowler's build and swing. Coaches treat it as a consistency
  check: the same laydown board every shot means the swing and footwork repeat.
- **Target.** Usually a board at the arrows ("I'm on 10"), sometimes the dots
  closer to the foul line or a spot farther down. Moving the eyes farther down
  the lane or closer is itself an adjustment ([USBC, Making In-Game
  Adjustments](https://bowl.com/making-in-game-adjustments)).
- **Breakpoint.** The farthest board the ball reaches toward the gutter before
  it hooks back, and how far down the lane that happens ("7 at 40"). Specto
  defines it as the ball position at the point farthest from the center of the
  lane ([Specto tutorial](https://www.spectobowling.com/tutorialperformance)).
- **The line.** Stand board, target and breakpoint together. "Playing 10 out to
  5" means crossing 10 at the arrows and breaking at 5. "Swinging it" or "out
  and back" means the ball heads toward the gutter after the arrows and comes
  back, which needs friction in the outside part of the lane. "Straight up 10"
  means a near-zero launch angle.
- **Hand position and release.** How far the hand sits around the side of the
  ball sets axis rotation, and so how much and how sharply the ball hooks. "Up
  the back" means less rotation and a smoother, earlier roll; more hand on the
  side means more rotation and a sharper move at the breakpoint
  ([National Bowling Academy, adjusting as lanes
  transition](https://www.nationalbowlingacademy.com/post/how-bowlers-can-adjust-as-lanes-transition)).

### How they adjust

The default order of adjustments for a league or college bowler is feet and
eyes first, then equipment, then speed and hand. The physical game is assumed
to be repeatable; adjustments are about the lane.

- **Move with the miss, same target.** Missed the pocket to the left, move the
  feet left and keep looking at the same board. This changes the angle through
  the front of the lane ([USBC](https://bowl.com/making-in-game-adjustments)).
- **2-and-1.** Feet two boards, target one board, same direction (standing 20
  looking at 10 becomes standing 22 looking at 11 in USBC's example). It changes
  the launch angle while keeping the ball near the same breakpoint, which is the
  point of the move ([NBA](https://www.nationalbowlingacademy.com/post/how-bowlers-can-adjust-as-lanes-transition)).
  Coaches call it a diagonal move.
- **3-and-1 and bigger.** A larger angle change, used once the lanes have broken
  down and the bowler needs to open the lane up. Bowlingball.com describes a
  dried-out head needing four or five boards with the feet and two with the mark
  ([bowlingball.com on oil
  breakdown](https://bowlingball.com/BowlVersity/what-is-bowling-lane-oil-break-down)).
  The ratios are rules of thumb, and how much a one-board move changes the
  ball's path at the pins depends on how far down the lane the target is
  ([BowlingThisMonth on target line
  adjustments](https://www.bowlingthismonth.com/bowling-tips/the-fundamentals-of-target-line-adjustments)).
- **Parallel move.** Feet and target move the same number of boards in the same
  direction. The line keeps its shape and shifts sideways
  ([USBC](https://bowl.com/making-in-game-adjustments)).
- **Moving left as the lane transitions.** Oil gets pushed down the lane and
  worn off the track area, the ball starts reading the lane earlier and hooks
  more, and right-handers move left a board or two at a time to find oil
  ([BowlingThisMonth, breakdown vs
  carrydown](https://www.bowlingthismonth.com/quick-tips/breakdown-vs-carrydown/)).
  The first sign on a good shot is often a 4 pin or a 3-6-10 left over
  ([NBA](https://www.nationalbowlingacademy.com/post/how-bowlers-can-adjust-as-lanes-transition)).
- **Ball changes.** A weaker or shinier ball skids farther and reads the lane
  later; a stronger or duller ball reads earlier. Bowlers carry several balls
  (their "arsenal") for this reason.
- **Speed changes.** Start 6 to 12 inches farther back and hold the ball lower
  to throw faster (shorter patterns, drying lanes); move up and hold it higher to
  throw slower (longer, heavier oil). "Foot speed dictates ball speed"
  ([NBA](https://www.nationalbowlingacademy.com/post/how-bowlers-can-adjust-as-lanes-transition)).
- **Hand and axis changes.** More rotation for more hook, up the back for less.
  These are the hardest to repeat under pressure, so they come last for most
  amateurs.

## 2. Lane geography

- **Boards.** 39 boards across. For a right-hander board 1 is next to the right
  gutter and board 20 is the center; a left-hander counts from the left
  ([gobowling.com arrow
  guide](https://gobowling.com/blog/bowling-arrow-guide-how-to-use-lane-arrows/)).
  Bowlers name half boards ("17 and a half") at the pocket and nowhere else.
- **Arrows.** Seven arrows on boards 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 and 35, set in a
  shallow V roughly 12 to 16 feet past the foul line, with the center arrow
  farthest down. Specto reads "arrows board" at 15 feet
  ([Specto](https://www.spectobowling.com/tutorialperformance)). Bowlers call
  them by number from their side: "second arrow" is the 10 board.
- **Dots.** Lane dots a few feet past the foul line (about 7 feet) on the same
  boards as the arrows, used for laydown and by some bowlers as a target.
  Approach dots sit at 12 and 15 feet behind the foul line for setting the
  stand board. Range finders are longer marks around 34 to 43 feet, used to
  read breakpoint distance
  ([gobowling.com](https://gobowling.com/blog/bowling-arrow-guide-how-to-use-lane-arrows/)).
- **Pocket.** The 1-3 for a right-hander, 1-2 for a left-hander. The ideal
  entry is the 17.5 board; USBC research reported an 85 percent strike rate
  entering at 17.5, with most of the rest single-pin leaves
  ([bowlingball.com, line up to the
  pocket](https://www.bowlingball.com/BowlVersity/line-up-to-the-bowling-pocket)).
  Specto gives the window as 16.5 to 17.5 ([Specto](https://www.spectobowling.com/tutorialperformance)).
  "High" means too close to the headpin (6 pin goes straight back past the 10),
  "light" means too far right (6 pin in the gutter, 10 left standing).
- **Oil patterns.** A house shot puts much more oil in the middle than on the
  outside, with ratios around 8:1 to 10:1 or more, and is about 40 feet long.
  It forgives misses: a ball missed right finds friction and comes back, a ball
  missed left finds oil and holds. A sport pattern is flatter, typically 4:1 or
  less under USBC's definition, so misses are punished
  ([USBC Sport Bowling](https://bowl.com/sport-bowling/information),
  [BowlerSmart on patterns](https://www.bowlersmart.com/tournaments/understanding-oil-patterns/)).
  Collegiate and tournament bowlers bowl on named sport patterns of
  32 to 50-plus feet.
- **Rule of 31.** Pattern length minus 31 gives a starting exit board: on a
  42-foot pattern, aim to have the ball at about the 11 board at about 42 feet
  ([Human Kinetics excerpt](https://shop.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/determining-the-breakpoint-using-the-rule-of-31)).
  Shorter patterns push the breakpoint toward the gutter and the foul line;
  longer patterns push it inside and toward the pins.
- **Transition.** Breakdown is oil removed from the track area; carrydown is oil
  dragged past the end of the pattern by the ball. Together they are
  "transition", and in league play it can start inside the first game
  ([bowlingball.com](https://bowlingball.com/BowlVersity/what-is-bowling-lane-oil-break-down),
  [Kegel on breakdown and carrydown](https://kegel.net/articles/oil-pattern-breakdown-and-carrydown-then-and-now)).
  Storm describes the arc as fresh, transition, burn
  ([Storm](https://www.stormbowling.com/page-building-7)).

## 3. What Specto measures and what counts as good

Specto (Kegel's in-center camera system) is the reference point serious
bowlers know. Its per-shot numbers, as defined in the [Specto
tutorial](https://www.spectobowling.com/tutorialperformance):

| Specto number | Definition | What coaches say is good |
|---|---|---|
| Foul line | board where the ball crossed the foul line | consistency, no fixed target |
| Arrows | board at 15 ft | hit any target from 1 to 25 on demand |
| Launch angle | direction in the front of the lane; 0 is straight, negative toward the gutter, positive toward the headpin | depends on the line being played |
| Breakpoint board and distance | farthest board from center, and how far down the lane | set by pattern, ball, speed and revs |
| Entry board | board at 59.5 ft | 16.5 to 17.5 |
| Impact angle | angle between 57 and 59.5 ft | 4 to 6 degrees (below) |
| Launch speed | speed in the front of the lane | pros 17.5 to 21 mph, shot-to-shot spread under 0.5 mph |
| Entry speed | speed at the pins | about 3 to 4 mph below launch speed |
| RPM | a calculated figure | Kegel itself calls it a reference only |

- **Entry angle.** 4 to 6 degrees is the commonly cited window for carry
  ([Wikipedia glossary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_bowling)). A
  2018 USBC study found that at 6 degrees the headpin contact zone that strikes
  more than 95 percent of the time is about twice as wide as at 2 degrees, and
  that anything above about 2 degrees requires a hook
  ([Physics Today](https://physicstoday.aip.org/quick-study/the-perfect-strike-in-tenpin-bowling)).
  Some sources warn that well above 6 degrees brings back-row leaves and the
  occasional split. Even advanced players cannot estimate their own entry angle
  without a measuring system ([Kegel, entry angle
  assessment](https://kegel.net/articles/bowling-pocket-entry-angle-assessment)),
  which is exactly why it is valuable on a phone.
- **Speed.** Bowlers quote launch speed, and they compare it against the
  scoreboard readout, which most centers show and which is usually measured
  somewhere down lane. That is a known source of "your app says 18, the screen
  says 16" confusion. The ball loses 3 to 4 mph on the way to the pins
  ([Specto](https://www.spectobowling.com/tutorialperformance)). A good impact
  speed is around 16 to 17.5 mph
  ([bowlingball.com, average ball
  speed](https://www.bowlingball.com/BowlVersity/an-understanding-of-average-bowling-ball-speed)).
- **Rev rate and axis.** Strokers under about 300 rpm, tweeners about 300 to
  400, crankers 400 and up; most league bowlers are tweeners
  ([BowlerSmart on ball
  motion](https://www.bowlersmart.com/2021/03/22/the-basics-of-bowling-ball-motion-surface-speed-rev-rate-tilt-and-rotation/),
  [Wikipedia, bowling form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_form)). Axis
  rotation is side roll; axis tilt is spinner-like tilt, and lower tilt reads
  the lane sooner. USBC warns that the labels are handed out without anyone
  measuring, and describes the tape-and-video way to measure revs properly
  ([USBC, What's Your Rev
  Rate](https://bowl.com/what-s-your-rev-rate)). Forum regulars distrust
  Specto's RPM and trust its speed and ball path
  ([Maverick Bowling forum](https://forum.maverickbowling.com/viewtopic.php?t=14002)).
  Rev rate matters most as a match to speed: a high-rev player with low speed
  hooks too early, a low-rev player with high speed hooks too late.
- **Which numbers they look at first.** In practice the order is speed first
  (everyone already knows theirs and it changes ball motion the most), then
  board at the arrows (did I hit my mark), then breakpoint and entry board (where
  did it go and did it reach the pocket), then entry angle (will it carry).
  Specto's own scoring for collegiate tryouts at Wichita State rated players on
  the range, not the average, of laydown, arrows, breakpoint, launch angle and
  launch speed over 15 shots
  ([Wichita State tryout document, since removed; seen in search
  results](https://www.wichita.edu/athletics/bowling/2020TryoutsWeekOneDescription.pdf)).
  Consistency is the number serious bowlers care about.

## 4. What serious amateurs and college bowlers practice and track

- **Scoring stats.** First-ball average, pocket percentage (first balls that
  hit the pocket), carry or strike-on-pocket-hit percentage, single-pin spare
  percentage, and conversion by specific leave
  ([Wichita State tryout stat
  description](https://www.wichita.edu/athletics/bowling/documents/Tryout_Stat_Description.pdf),
  [BowlingThisMonth on analysis
  data](https://bowlingthismonth.com/bowling-tips/how-to-arrive-at-more-meaningful-analysis-data),
  [BowlingThisMonth on spare
  data](https://www.bowlingthismonth.com/bowling-tips/collecting-and-analyzing-purposeful-spare-shooting-data)).
  A first-ball average over 9.2 suggests most shots are reaching the pocket.
  Kegel's practice advice is to log which side you miss on and which pins you
  leave on pocket hits, and to look at roll, speed or equipment if you strike on
  under half of pocket hits
  ([Kegel, how to practice](https://www.kegel.net/white-papers-articles2/2016/3/14/wiosmh5u55we37yqf7ijrw8ah6gy6v)).
  LaneTalk already owns this scoring side.
- **Ball-motion practice.** Repeat one line and see how tight the numbers are:
  same target, same speed, same breakpoint. Then make a move (2-and-1, speed up,
  ball change) and compare before and after. Spare shooting at the corner pins
  with a straighter ball, often with a plastic ball, is a staple of every
  college practice plan.
- **Language in reviews and forums.** Shot, line, boards, arrows, mark,
  breakpoint, launch board, entry, pocket, high, light, carry, rev rate, speed,
  ball motion, "reading the lanes", "the lanes broke down", "had to move in".
  LaneTrax reviewers say "launch board", "break point" (two words) and "entry"
  ([LaneTrax reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lanetrax/id6475736816?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)).
- **Common complaints about tracking apps.**
  - Lane and foul-line detection is finicky and does not recover after the
    phone gets bumped.
  - Numbers that don't match what the video shows.
  - Rev rate that is estimated rather than measured, or measured too late.
  - Shots that silently fail to track.
  - Subscriptions; LaneTrax users ask for a one-time purchase.
  - Data stuck at the lanes with no way to review or compare at home (the
    common Specto complaint,
    [Specto App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/specto-bowling/id1180311427)).

## 5. Implications for TrueLine

**Frame every result as "was it me or the lane".** This is the decision a
bowler makes after every shot. If the ball crossed the intended target and
still missed the pocket, the lane changed and it is time to move. If it missed
the target, it was execution and moving would chase a bad shot. TrueLine can
answer this directly if the bowler can tell it their target board. A per-session
"target" setting, and a result that says "crossed 11, your mark was 10", would
be more useful to this audience than any new metric.

**Lead with speed and board at the arrows, then breakpoint, then entry board and
angle.** Speed and arrows are what bowlers act on first. Entry board and angle
explain carry. All of them should read as boards and feet the way bowlers say
them: "7 at 41 ft", "17.5", "5.2 degrees".

**Show consistency before single shots.** Session spread (speed range,
arrows range, breakpoint range) is the professional measure. Pros hold launch
speed within half a mph. A session view that shows "speed 16.8 to 17.3" says
more than a list of throws.

**Show breakpoint drift across a session.** A breakpoint creeping toward the
pocket on the same target is transition, the cue to move left. Plotting
breakpoint board by shot number is a cheap, very bowler-literate feature.

**Terms to use.** Board, arrows, target or mark, breakpoint, entry board,
entry angle, pocket, launch speed, launch angle, line, shot, move (as in "make a
move"), left-hander and right-hander. "Breakpoint 7 at 41 ft" rather than two
tiles labeled "Breakpoint" and "Breakpoint Distance".

**Terms to avoid.** "Hook Factor" in boards per second squared (no bowler has a
reference for it; if hook stays, report it as boards of hook from breakpoint to
entry, the way bowlers count it), trajectory, deviation, lateral offset, curve,
aim, and decimals past one place on boards (17.5 yes, 17.53 no). Avoid "pro" or
board-level accuracy claims. Don't say "rev rate" anywhere until it ships, and
when it does, be explicit that it is measured, since that is the trust gap with
Specto.

**Speed needs a footnote.** Say which speed it is (launch speed, front of the
lane) so bowlers don't think the app disagrees with the scoreboard.

**Calibration and failure honesty are selling points.** The complaints above
are TrueLine's 1.1 features: drift checks after a bump, explicit "couldn't
track this shot and here is why", throws that don't count when they fail, a
one-time price, and CSV export for review at home.

**Handedness everywhere.** Board numbers, the pocket (1-2) and launch angle sign
must mirror for left-handers, in copy as well as math.

## 6. App Store subtitle and keywords for 1.1

### How search reads the listing

Apple indexes the app name, subtitle and keyword field, plus the category
([Apple, App Store search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)). The
keyword field is 100 characters, commas with no spaces, and Apple says not to
repeat words from the name, subtitle or category, not to add plurals of words
already present, and not to use trademarks or competing app names
([Apple, product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)).
The subtitle (30 characters) should name features or typical uses that
resonate with the audience. ASO practice holds that words combine across the
three fields, so "speed" in the subtitle plus "bowling" and "ball" in the name
covers "bowling ball speed", and that singular covers plural
([AppTweak on plurals](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/do-singular-or-plural-keywords-rank-differently-in-aso)).

Name, fixed: **TrueLine: Bowling Ball Tracker**. That already covers bowling,
ball and tracker.

| BowlTrace: Bowling Analysis | Trajectory, speed & rev rate |

| Tenpin Toolkit: Bowling Tools | Making you a better bowler |
| SPECTO Bowling | (none shown) |
| TrueLine 1.0 (live) | Speed, hook & entry angle |

Nobody else leads with entry angle, and most of the field is three-item lists.
Speed is the one ball-motion word bowlers search with most, because every
center's scoreboard shows it and it is what new tracking-app users want first.

### Ranked subtitle shortlist

1. **Speed and entry angle per shot** (30). The two numbers a bowler would name
   first and last: how hard it was thrown and how it hit the pocket. Two items,
   not a list. "Speed" goes back into the subtitle where it combines with the
   name into "bowling ball speed", the most likely search in this niche. "Shot"
   is the word bowlers use for a delivery and its data ("shot data", "good
   shot"). Top pick.
2. **Entry angle on every throw** (26). The current pick. Distinct and readable,
   but it drops speed from the strongest field, and "throw" and "every" add
   little search value. Fine if Adam prefers to lead with the one number nobody
   else shows.
3. **Speed and entry angle on video** (30). Same pair as the top pick, with
   "video" indexed in the subtitle instead of "shot". Reads more like a feature
   description than a bowler talking. If chosen, move "shot" into the keywords
   in place of "video".
4. **Your speed and entry angle** (26). Plainest version. Leaves 4 characters
   unused and indexes nothing beyond the top pick.
5. **Know your line to the pocket** (28). The most bowler-sounding phrase, but
   "line" and "pocket" are weak search terms, and it doesn't say what the app
   measures.

When rev rate ships, "Speed and rev rate per shot" (27) is the natural
successor, with entry angle moving to the keywords.

### Keyword string for the top pick

```
hook,arrows,breakpoint,pocket,board,launch,league,practice,coach,stats,video,lane,tenpin,analysis
```

97 characters, 14 terms. No word repeats the name (TrueLine, bowling, ball,
tracker) or the subtitle (speed, entry, angle, per, shot). No trademarks.

Why each term is there: hook, arrows, breakpoint, pocket, board and launch are
the words bowlers use for the line and the shot, and they combine with "angle",
"speed" and "ball" into real queries ("launch angle", "hook ball", "pocket
angle"). League and practice describe when the app is used; coach, stats and
analysis catch the generic improvement searches that LaneTrax and BowlTrace
compete for; video and lane describe the setup; tenpin catches non-US
spelling.

Left out on purpose: rev (not shipped, an irrelevant term is a rejection risk
and would bring disappointed users), strike and spare (they pull in score
keepers and games), oil and pattern (the app doesn't read the oil), trainer
(overlaps coach), pin and mph (low value for the space), and USBC, PBA and
Specto (other parties' marks).
